Abstract: In this talk, we will focus on the intersection of graph theory, algebra, and probability theory. Recent research in spectral graph theory and its applications
have highlighted the utility of probabilistic approaches.
We will take time to cover some interesting methods to
motivate research questions. There will be a live-code
demonstration that showcases implementation of these
methods and what computation in mathematics research
can look like. We will discuss eigenvector approximation,
clustering, and reconstructibility of graphs. Focusing
largely on spectral graph theory, the interdisciplinary
nature of the talk will offer fun perspective into intuitive
problems.
